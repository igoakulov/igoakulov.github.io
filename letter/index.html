As a product manager, I once wanted to grow into an engineer to create more value. With agents, engineers are now managing products, and steering has become the shiny new hard skill. Bingo.

I’ve coordinated work and managed teams of different sizes and compositions, on-site and remotely. Steering people now carries over to agents unsurprisingly well.

I’m also a generalist with a background in many domains, and I can explore a problem space well. I think it’s mostly pattern recognition and a grasp of adjacent things that let me seek out the rough shape of a problem or solution quickly. With LLMs, it’s now 20x faster. I’ve also dropped my old ways: I now design, prototype and iterate in code, and I’ve adjusted my habits to write and design for agents and humans alike.

At my previous company, AppXite (B2B SaaS for growing a subscription business), I led product to PMF in 2 strategic segments and 3.5x ARR. Most of my work went into the 0-to-1 areas of CPQ and partner channels, as well as the conventional core: pricing, subscriptions, billing and invoicing. We shipped over 130 sprints of updates with hundreds of useful features. I effectively worked as both lead and IC:

  • Planned and prioritized our roadmap with the CEO and heads of departments and domains.
  • Managed the work of 20+ people: planned and ran sprints, removed roadblocks and team friction, handled releases.
  • Did analysis and design for most features, and ran exploratory research and user interviews where needed. Authored most PRDs and user stories. When the product grew too big, I standardized my work into a process for others.
  • Helped grow relevant competencies in the company: managed a PM and a BA, and coached many non-reports from Engineering, QA, CSM and other teams on product, design, customers and business, and how to work with each other.

I uphold several fundamental principles for all this to work. I’m intellectually honest, and I pursue and respect truth above all. I need a high-quality argument to be convinced, but I also seek out my own blind spots. That’s why I make decisions based on probabilities rather than certainty, and I’m eager to disagree and commit.

Let’s work.
